# CO2-rSO2 Literature and Positioning Memo

Date searched: 2026-06-08

This memo uses current web searches plus the local CO2 analysis package. It is meant to guide manuscript framing, not to replace a formal systematic review.

## Literature anchors

1. NIRS is established as a continuous cerebral oxygenation monitor in cardiac surgery, but baseline values vary widely and outcome links remain inconsistent. Chan et al. reviewed 11 RCTs with 953 participants and reported a pooled baseline SctO2 of 66.4% with a broad reference range. Source: https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/near-infrared-spectroscopy-in-adult-cardiac-surgery-patients-a-sy/

2. Carbon dioxide manipulation can change cerebral oxygenation under controlled conditions. Mutch et al. reported a graded cerebral saturation response to end-tidal CO2 in healthy adults using controlled gas targeting. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23469096/

3. Quantitative EtCO2-rSO2 modeling has been performed in carotid endarterectomy under general anesthesia, including continuous EtCO2 and rSO2 measurements and pharmacodynamic modeling. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5777433/

4. Cardiac-surgery studies have evaluated whether NIRS can reflect CO2-related physiology. Park et al. studied the correlation between changes in arterial CO2 tension and regional cerebral oxygen saturation during cardiac surgery. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21844113/

5. Targeted mild hypercapnia has been tested in cardiac surgery settings, including a pilot randomized study during cardiopulmonary bypass and a more recent off-pump CABG pilot. These trials support biological plausibility but do not replace the current observational high-frequency modeling question. Sources: https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/a-pilot-randomized-controlled-study-of-mild-hypercapnia-during-ca and https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053077024001563

6. Recent NIRS work in cardiac surgery continues to emphasize continuous, noninvasive monitoring, but often focuses on device validation or monitoring algorithms rather than adjusted EtCO2 response curves. Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-03527-x

## Gap statement for the CO2 paper

The literature supports three premises:

- cerebral oxygenation is clinically monitored during cardiac surgery;
- CO2 can influence cerebral oxygenation through cerebrovascular physiology;
- EtCO2 is continuously available but physiologically mixed, reflecting ventilation and perfusion.

The current manuscript should fill this gap:

> A large, high-frequency, adjusted intraoperative analysis quantifying the nonlinear EtCO2-rSO2 relationship during off-pump coronary bypass surgery, with simultaneous comparison against FiO2 and temperature.

## Claims that are defensible now

- EtCO2 has the largest current clinical-step association with left and right cerebral rSO2 among EtCO2, FiO2, and temperature.
- The EtCO2-rSO2 relationship is nonlinear and channel-specific.
- EtCO2 should be considered when interpreting intraoperative cerebral oxygenation changes.
- The analysis supports prospective testing of EtCO2-guided oxygenation management.

## Claims that are not defensible yet

- EtCO2 causes higher rSO2.
- Mild hypercapnia improves neurological outcomes in this cohort.
- A specific EtCO2 target should be used clinically.
- EtCO2 is more important than MAP, SV, HR, RRtotal, TVinsp, or Pmean, because those all-intraop comparisons are currently incomplete.

## Best target-journal posture

Nature Communications is possible only if the manuscript tells a broad physiologic story and provides complete sensitivity analysis, clean figures, source data, code availability, and a strong explanation of why high-frequency intraoperative modeling changes understanding. A specialty anesthesia/cardiac surgery journal is safer if the final scope remains physiology-focused without outcomes.

Practical recommendation:

- Draft in Nature-style first for clarity and argument strength.
- Decide target journal after sensitivity runs and figure cleanup.
